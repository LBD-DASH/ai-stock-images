### (c) Adobe Stock generative AI rules (current, checked 27 Sep 2026; page last updated 11 Jun 2026)
Source: [Adobe Stock generative AI content guidelines](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/submit-your-content/submit-generative-ai-content/generative-ai-content-guidelines.html), [Generative AI FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/generative-ai-faq.html).

- Always tick **"Created using generative AI tools"** in the Contributor Portal for every AI-made upload, regardless of whether a model release is also needed.
- Since June 2023, Adobe no longer requires "generative AI" in the **title** — write a normal descriptive title, no prompt jargon, no technical parameters.
- Add **"generative AI"** as one **keyword**, in English, even if the rest of the keywords are in another language.
- Prompts, titles and keywords must never include: artist names, real people's names, fictional character names, references to copyrighted works, government agency names, other third-party IP, or anything implying a real newsworthy event. Violations can mean content removal or account termination.
- Submit **photorealistic** AI images as content type "Photos"; anything illustrative or fantastical (impossible scenes, stylised) as "Illustrations".
- If the image depicts or is based on a real, identifiable person, a **model release is required**. If it merely *resembles* a person without being based on one, tick **"People and Property are fictional"** instead.
- You must hold the rights to submit — check the generator's own terms confirm the right to submit its outputs to Adobe Stock for commercial licensing (this is the gap flagged in (b) above for Midjourney/Freepik).
- Quality/spam rule: don't submit multiple near-identical iterations of the same prompt; each upload should add unique value to the collection — relevant given how many prompt variants are in this log.
- Adobe's guidance page is dated 11 Jun 2026, so it's worth a quick re-check for changes right before the first real upload, not just once now.
